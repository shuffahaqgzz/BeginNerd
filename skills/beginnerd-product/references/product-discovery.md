# PRODUCT-FOUNDATION-01 — Product Discovery and Validation

**Trigger:** The user has a product idea, uncertain target users, unverified assumptions, or a problem to investigate. Do not force a full market study for a small, well-understood internal change.

## Evidence-aware discovery
1. Identify the affected users, current situation, specific struggle, workaround, consequence, and desired outcome. Inspect existing product documents and research before asking the same questions again.
2. Separate **observed facts**, user/Owner reports, **assumptions**, product hypotheses, and decisions. The Owner's opinion is not evidence that prospective users experience a problem.
3. When the problem is uncertain or material, recommend a proportionate **non-leading problem interview**:
   - Ask what happened the *last time* a real situation occurred, not whether someone would like an imagined feature.
   - Ask about job/context, trigger, current workaround, frequency, consequence, existing alternatives, and what success would look like.
   - Use a lightweight Jobs-to-be-Done framing when useful: *When [situation], I need to [make progress], so I can [desired outcome].*
   - Obtain appropriate permission and respect privacy before contacting people; agent-generated interview text is **not a completed interview**.
4. Inventory existing alternatives and, when relevant, competitors. Capture verified source/date, product scope, strengths, limitations, switching costs, pricing claims and user context. Do not invent customers or claim a competitor's pricing is current without evidence.
5. Turn the biggest uncertain assumption into an **experiment proposal**: hypothesis, target participant/segment, method (interview, prototype observation, concierge trial, landing-page test or an appropriate simpler test), observable metric or qualitative acceptance, evidence source and a decision threshold **proposed for Owner approval**. Never invent baseline, numbers or confidence.
6. Explain the smallest useful next validation step. Group only the material product decisions for approval; don't require research rituals or repeated approvals for every question.
7. Record actual evidence and its limitations in PRODUCT.md (Lean) or PRODUCT-BRIEF.md (Structured). Use references/documentation-and-change-control.md for authorized file writes.
8. Revisit validated/invalidated assumptions after real feedback from the released product. Route accepted scope refinements to PRODUCT-FOUNDATION-02/03; implementation tasks still belong to Planning.

## Outcome
A human-readable problem statement, known target audience, evidence/assumption separation, next highest-value hypothesis, proposed validation method and product outcome. Label interviews and experiments **PLANNED** until they actually occur. Do not claim product-market fit or customer validation from a drafted brief.

**Boundary:** This is not final technology selection (SOLUTION-06) or task decomposition (PLANNING-02).
